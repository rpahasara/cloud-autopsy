**Assumptions not covered by the Phase 1C documents** (provisional, needing architecture review):
- complete batches need at least one observation, and empty batches have none;
- partial coverage is never counted as verified;
- a batch conflict excludes every variant;
- a broken cursor chain stops verified coverage at the break;
- any gap makes the manifest `incomplete`;
- a root manifest larger than the 1 MiB canonical ceiling is rejected (since 0.2 the root holds only page commitments, so this bound is not reached at the provisional ceilings).

The sections below describe version 0.1. The [review follow-up](#review-follow-up) describes what changed in 0.2 and takes precedence where they differ.

**Batch outcomes:**

| Outcome | Coverage | Observations | Reason | What it proves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| complete | Verified interval inside the window | At least one | None | Every observation the query returned for that interval |
| empty | Verified interval | None | None | Only that this query returned nothing within that coverage |
| partial | Declared, not verified | Any | rate_limited, timeout, page_limit, response_too_large or continuity_lost; truncation allowed | Nothing about what is missing |
| failed | None | None | rate_limited, timeout, source_unavailable or invalid_response | Nothing; never empty |
| denied | None | None | authorization_denied | Nothing; never empty |

**Batch identity and idempotency:**
- A batch is identified by (source, query, `batch_key`). Its digest covers every field except `delivery.attempt`.
- An identical redelivery is dropped.
- A different batch under the same key is a **batch conflict**: every variant is excluded, along with its observations and coverage, and reported.

**Cursor chain:** `sequence` runs from 1 with no gaps. Batch 1 starts from a `null` cursor, and each later batch's `cursor.before` must equal the previous batch's `cursor.after`. Coverage after a break isn't verified.

## Gap policy and sealing

**For each planned acquisition, the window outside verified coverage becomes gaps:**

| Situation | Gap type | Reasons | Acquisition status |
|---|---|---|---|
| No batches | missing | no_acquisition | missing |
| Only conflicting batches | unverifiable | batch_conflict | unverifiable |
| A failed or denied batch | failed | the batch reasons | failed or denied when nothing verified, else partial |
| A truncated batch | truncated | page_limit or response_too_large, plus truncated | partial |
| Partial batches, a broken cursor chain or a batch conflict | unverifiable | the specific reasons | partial |
| A hole between clean batches | missing | coverage_gap | partial |

**Other rules:**
- Each batch conflict also gets its own `unverifiable` gap listing the conflicting batch digests.
- **Evidence:** every observation from non-conflicting batches goes through the CA-P2-002c `EvidenceLedger`. Only authoritative identities become members, as `{evidence_id, content_digest}` sorted by ID. A conflicted identity is excluded and reported as an `unverifiable`/`identity_conflict` gap with its content digests.
- **Status:** `complete` only when there are no gaps; otherwise `incomplete`. Limitations are sorted codes: `mandatory_acquisition_incomplete`, `optional_acquisition_incomplete` and `identity_conflicts_excluded`. A partial investigation can therefore be sealed, but only in a form that says so.
- **Limits:** per-batch observations and bytes, batches per acquisition, total observations and total bytes. Exceeding any limit rejects with `limit_exceeded`; nothing is dropped silently.
- **Registry pin:** the registry must match the plan's pinned ID and digest at plan decoding, at sealing and at verification. Any mismatch gives `registry_mismatch`.
- **Determinism:** reordering, duplicating and retrying identical batches, including different delivery attempts, produces byte-identical manifests.
- **Excluded from the manifest:** delivery attempts, arrival order, duplicate counts, ledger delivery counts, run IDs and wall-clock times. These are listed in `excluded_from_digest`. Evidence appears only as IDs and digests, so no raw payload, credential or ground truth can enter. Plans and batches are closed schemas, so fields like `ground_truth`, `api_token` and `query` are rejected.
- **Verification:** `verify(record, plan, registry)` checks the exact field set, the version, the manifest digest and the plan and registry pins. `verify_replay(record, plan, registry, batches)` re-seals the retained batches and requires identical bytes (`replay_mismatch` otherwise).
- **Immutability:** a `FrozenInput` holds immutable canonical bytes, and `to_record()` returns a fresh copy.

## Known limitations and unresolved decisions

1. **Manifest size:** resolved in 0.2 by paging (see the [review follow-up](#review-follow-up)).
2. **Invalid batch handling:** one malformed batch rejects the whole seal with a typed error. Whether to quarantine an invalid batch as a gap instead needs a decision. It needs reliable attribution of the batch to an acquisition.
3. **Unverified evidence:** resolved in 0.2. Members carry `verified` or `unverified` coverage, and provenance is recorded per batch. Whether downstream rules may use unverified members at all is a CA-P2-005 decision.
4. **Coverage precision:** coverage bounds must be exact milliseconds. Sources with sub-millisecond bounds would need rounding rules.
5. **Opaque rule versions:** `rule_versions` are format-checked strings until engine rules exist (CA-P2-005).
6. **Availability:** `availability_generation` is always 0; lifecycle overlays (CA-P2-006) aren't modeled.
7. **Pure functions only:** there is no transaction, lease, compare-and-swap or concurrent-seal protection. Atomic sealing is CA-P2-003.

## Required before PostgreSQL work (CA-P2-003)

CA-P2-003 has not been started. Its backlog entry depends on CA-P2-002 plus "supported DB/image pin and lifecycle design approved". Concretely:

1. **Authorization:** product authorizes CA-P2-003 as a named item (Gate I), or explicitly extends the preparatory direction.
2. **Contract review:** independent review of CA-P2-002a–d, or an explicit decision to build on provisional contracts. The identity, digest and manifest bytes would become database keys and constraints.
3. **Pins (CA-DEC-002):** the exact PostgreSQL 18 minor version and container image digest, the Python driver (candidate psycopg 3) with license, hash pin and advisory check, the migration approach, and the packaging and lock decision (D5). These are the first third-party dependencies, so the dependency policy, inventory, SBOM and advisory service (D6) must be in place.
4. **Database roles:** runtime roles and least privilege (no superuser at runtime), plus how credentials are provisioned outside Git for a local synthetic database.
5. **Decisions from this slice:**
   - how the paged collections map to tables (for example `run_evidence` with a coverage column, a provenance relation and manifest pages stored by digest);
   - invalid-batch handling;
   - how conflict quarantine maps to tables;
   - the at-least-once ingest idempotency horizon (CA-DEC-008 open question).
6. **Test environment:** local, synthetic and disposable, for example a pinned container, with no shared or remote database. This needs approval because it adds infrastructure.

## <a id="review-follow-up"></a>Review follow-up (PR #19): unresolved batches, resource accounting and paging

### Confirmed defects at `3930740`

Each was reproduced with the 0.1 API before the fix, and `test_frozen_input_review.py` keeps them as regressions.

| ID | Defect | Reproduction at 3930740 |
|---|---|---|
| R1 (P1) | An acquisition was `complete` whenever clean batches covered the window, even with an unresolved batch present | Adding a partial (timeout), failed (source_unavailable) or out-of-chain batch to fully covered deployments still gave manifest `complete`, acquisition `complete` and 0 gaps |
| R2 | Observations from partial or out-of-chain batches became members without qualification | Members had only `evidence_id` and `content_digest` |
| R3 | Conflicting batch variants were excluded before the total limits were applied | `max_total_bytes`, `max_observations_total` and `max_batches_per_acquisition` were each bypassed by a conflicting variant |
| R4 | Delivered records were unbounded | 500 duplicate deliveries were all decoded; there was no delivery limit |
| R5 | Manifest capacity | One document holds roughly 6,000–7,000 members, below CA-DEC-007's provisional 10,000 records |

### Fixes and changed semantics (gap policy 0.2)

- **R1:**
  - A batch is clean only when it is complete or empty, untruncated and in the cursor chain.
  - An acquisition is `complete` or `empty` only if clean coverage spans the window **and every batch is clean** and there are no conflicts.
  - Every unresolved batch gets its own gap with subject `batch` (failed, truncated or unverifiable), even when other batches cover the window. Gaps now have a `subject`: `coverage`, `batch` or `identity`.
- **R2:**
  - Members carry `coverage: verified` if any delivering batch is clean, otherwise `unverified`, plus a `batch_count`.
  - The `evidence_provenance` collection records each (evidence, content digest, batch) delivery.
  - The limitation `unverified_evidence_included` is added when any member is unverified.
  - Verified coverage (`coverage` collection, `verified_ms`), evidence availability (`availability_generation`) and membership stay separate fields.
  - Policy: unverified evidence is kept and labeled rather than excluded, so that later rules (CA-P2-005) can decide. Excluding it would hide observed facts.
- **R3 and R4:**
  - Deliveries are consumed lazily, so a generator works.
  - Each delivered record counts toward the new plan limit `max_batch_deliveries` (ceiling 10,000) **before** decoding.
  - Each distinct variant, including conflicting ones, counts toward the per-acquisition, observation and byte limits as soon as it is seen.
  - Memory is bounded by the plan limits, because only distinct variants are retained.
- **Duplicates:** identical redeliveries are idempotent and never create gaps. This is tested by sealing every scenario with each batch delivered three times.

### Paged manifest (R5)

**Evaluated options:**

| Option | Capacity | Complexity | Fit with Phase 1C and the PostgreSQL model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raise the canonical ceiling for manifests | Linear in bytes; one document | Low | Weakens the bounded-parse guarantee for every consumer; one large JSONB value |
| Root plus Merkle root, membership only in the database | Unbounded | Medium | Verification needs the database; the manifest is no longer self-contained replay evidence |
| **Root plus content-addressed pages (chosen)** | 100,000 members at about 250 KB of root | Medium | Pages map to a `manifest_page (run_id, collection, page_index, digest, bytes)` relation and `run_evidence` rows; every page is bounded by the existing canonical ceiling |

The paging added about 200 lines, mostly verification. That is proportionate given the CA-DEC-007 workload, so the smaller alternatives were not taken.

**Design:**
- **Collections:** `batches`, `coverage`, `evidence`, `evidence_provenance` and `gaps`. Each is sorted by a unique key and split greedily into pages of at most 1,000 items and 262,144 canonical bytes (provisional).
- **Pages:** each page is `{contract_version: ca-frozen-input-page/0.1-provisional, collection, page_index, items}`, and its digest is `ca-frozen-input-page-sha256/0.1-provisional:<SHA-256>`.
- **Root:** commits, per collection, `item_count` and the ordered list of `{index, item_count, digest}`. Key ranges aren't repeated in the root; the first design had them, and that would have exceeded 1 MiB at the ceilings.
- **Identity gaps:** a gap for a conflicted identity carries a `variant_count`. The variants themselves are in `evidence_provenance`, so no single item can grow without bound.

**Verification levels:**
- `verify_root(root, plan, registry, expected_digest=None)` checks exact fields, the version, the root digest, the plan and registry pins, supported page parameters, and well-formed commitments (indexes 0..n−1, bounded counts, counts summing to `item_count`). It does **not** prove that pages exist.
- `verify_full(root, pages, ...)` requires every committed page and rejects:
  - any supplied page that isn't committed (`page_unexpected`);
  - any page that occupies a committed slot with different bytes (`page_digest_mismatch`);
  - a missing page (`page_missing`);
  - a header, field set, size or count mismatch, or items out of order or duplicated within or across pages (`page_invalid`).

  An identical page supplied twice is tolerated.
- A root is self-certifying, so an attacker able to recompute every digest could rebuild a consistent manifest over fewer pages. Callers bind verification to the digest published or stored at seal time with `expected_digest`. Without it, such a forgery is caught only by replay.
- `verify_replay(...)` re-seals the retained batches and requires identical root and page bytes.

### Capacity evidence

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| 10,000 synthetic members, end to end (10 batches of 1,000, about 520 KB each) | Seal about 3.8 s, full verification about 0.6 s on the development machine (not a benchmark). Root 19.5 KB; 11 evidence pages and 15 provenance pages, each at most 262,144 bytes and 1,000 items |
| 10,000 members, shuffled and partly redelivered | Byte-identical root and pages |
| Incomplete page sets | Dropping the first, middle or last evidence page fails with `page_missing`; a recomputed root over the reduced set fails `page_invalid` (counts) or `manifest_digest_mismatch` (against the sealed digest) |
| Structural forgeries with recomputed digests | Duplicate across pages, unsorted within a page, duplicate within a page, extra field, swapped pages and an overfull page all fail with `page_invalid` |
| 100,000 members (the `max_observations_total` ceiling), paging layer | All pages within bounds; counts sum correctly |
| Worst-case root at all ceilings (64 acquisitions × 1,000 batches, 100,000 observations, three times the page estimate) | Under the 1 MiB canonical ceiling |
| Largest single item (a batch-conflict gap with 1,000 digests) | Fits one page |

### Remaining risks

- **Thresholds are unreviewed:** page bounds (1,000 items, 256 KiB) and `max_batch_deliveries` (10,000).
- **Re-forging:** binding to the sealed digest is the caller's responsibility (`expected_digest`). Storage-level immutability is CA-P2-003.
- **Seal cost:** sealing is single-threaded and keeps all distinct batches in memory, bounded by plan limits. At 100,000 observations an end-to-end seal would take an estimated 40 seconds or more on the development machine, which is unmeasured.
- **Invalid batches:** a single malformed batch still rejects the whole seal.
- **Self-authored tests:** the 10,000-member tests use generated metric samples and the vectors are implementer-authored, so independent review is still needed.
